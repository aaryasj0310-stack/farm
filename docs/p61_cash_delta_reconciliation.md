## 2. Analysis of Direct Product Lift

1. **High-Value Product Capture**:
   - Treatment achieved modest volume increases in Milk (+1.97 u), Melon (+2.45 u), and Strawberry (+0.68 u).
   - These incremental units directly contributed **+$1,450.45** in gross high-margin revenue.
   - Crucially, average realized prices remained virtually unchanged:
     - Milk: \$238.91 -> \$241.23
     - Melon: \$238.84 -> \$238.30
     - Strawberry: \$250.34 -> \$249.63
   - This proves that **the market easily absorbed the incremental sales without price depression**.

2. **Wheat Revenue Reduction (-$731.49)**:
   - Treatment sold 24.24 fewer units of wheat over the season.
   - This was an intended direct consequence of the **safe wheat floor enforcement**: storage hygiene prioritized high-value goods over wheat, preserving wheat for animal feed buffer. This reduced wheat gross sales by \$731.49 but protected herd safety completely.

3. **Working Capital Compounding (+778.16)**:
   - On days where late-evening sales successfully cleared shed space before major dumps (e.g. Day 10, Day 14), cash was banked 1–2 days earlier.
   - Having cash earlier prevented cash-constrained seed order trimming and avoided secondary purchase penalties.

## 3. Why the Cash Lift Fell Short of the \$2,000 Threshold

The recoverable-value ledger in P6 estimated a theoretical unconstrained pool of \$4,300–\$6,026 and hypothesized recoverable cash of +\$2,500 to +\$4,000.

The actual realized lift of **+$1,439.86** captured only ~24% of the reference discard value.

**The shortfall is explained by a single structural constraint**:
- Storage hygiene could only sell inventory physically present in the shed.
- Because 75–85% of overflow volume arrived from worker backpacks at midnight rather than sitting in the shed at Hour 20–22, ~97% of discards continued unabated.
- The +\$1,439.86 captured essentially 100% of the value available in the shed, but the shed was simply not where the bulk of the unliquidated goods were located.
